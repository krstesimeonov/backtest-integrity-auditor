# Edge Audit — Synthetic Swing Trader A

> **Sample deliverable on synthetic data.** "Synthetic Swing Trader A", the instruments ALPHA
> and BETA and every trade below were generated by `make_sample.py`; no real person, account
> or market is involved. What was planted is listed in `README.md`.

**Period audited:** 6 January 2025 to 25 February 2026, 420 closed trades on ALPHA and BETA
**Your declaration:** 12 strategy variants tried before this one
**Report:** `output/edge_audit_report.md` (full tables) and `output/edge_audit_report.json`

## Summary

1. **No reliable evidence of an edge once your 12 variants are counted.** After costs the
   average trade made USD 5.07 (95 % interval 0.65 to 9.45), a per-trade Sharpe ratio of
   0.109 and a t-statistic of 2.22. Had this been the only variant you ever tested, that
   would pass our test (DSR 0.987). But the best of 12 variants with no skill at all would be
   expected to reach a Sharpe ratio of about 0.081 by luck, and against that bar the DSR is
   0.711, below the 0.95 we require. At the present Sharpe ratio about 931 trades would be
   needed; you have 420.

2. **Your skill is in the entries.** Entering when you did earned USD 12.26 per trade
   (interval 10.82 to 13.80) more than entering at a random time on the same day. None of
   2,000 simulated random-entry histories did as well as your actual entries (p = 0.0005,
   the smallest value 2,000 simulations can give). Exit timing (USD 0.23 per trade, p = 0.37)
   and the direction of your trades (USD −1.97 per trade, p = 0.84) are indistinguishable
   from chance.

3. **Results deteriorated late in the period.** Two of our four monitors flagged it: the
   50-trade rolling average at trade 344 (8 December 2025) and Page–Hinkley at trade 367
   (1 January 2026); CUSUM and the sequential test stayed below their thresholds. Each monitor
   is set so that, had nothing changed, an alarm within 1,000 monitored trades would have had
   a 5 % chance; over the 280 trades watched here that chance is at most 1.4 % for any one of
   them. The 50-trade rolling average went from a high of USD 23.11 to USD −4.81 at the end of
   the log. The monitors watch the total result per trade; this audit does not show which
   part of the result declined.

4. **The cost margin is thin.** Execution against the prevailing prices cost USD 2.18 per
   trade and fees 3.00 bps of notional per round trip. The strategy breaks even at
   4.64 bps of additional cost (interval 0.60 to 8.78).

5. **No other effect we could detect.** Position sizing (permutation p = 0.056), holding
   period (p = 0.52) and volatility regime (p = 0.090) show no reliable influence on results.

## What we recommend

* Do not add risk to this strategy on the strength of its overall record: at 12 variants the
  record is not distinguishable from the best of 12 lucky ones.
* The entries are the asset: entry timing is the only skill the audit found. Look at them
  first when asking what changed in late 2025. An alarm marks when the evidence of a
  deterioration became strong enough, so a real change would have begun before the alarms.
* Treat execution as a lever: the USD 2.18 per trade lost to fills equals 43 % of the
  average net result of USD 5.07.
* Re-audit after a further block of trades with the entry rule unchanged, declaring the
  same 12 variants (plus any new ones).

## What we did

We checked the log against the trade-log schema `edge-audit-trades/1.0` (no errors), matched
every trade to 30-minute prices for its instrument, and ran the seven V4 methods described
in `docs/edge_audit/DESIGN.md`: bootstrap intervals, luck versus skill with your declared
trial count, timing decomposition, holding-period and volatility terciles, cost sensitivity,
decay monitoring and the fixed-size counterfactual. Intervals are 95 % bootstrap intervals
over trades (2,000 resamples); every random step uses seed 20261103, so the report can be
reproduced exactly.

## Limits of this audit

* Up to 8 trades were open at the same time. Overlapping trades share market moves, so the
  intervals above are somewhat narrower than they should be.
* The trial count is your declaration. If more variants were tried, the bar rises further.
* The monitors say when the evidence of a deterioration became strong enough, not when it
  began, and not which part of the result changed. Their 5 % false-alarm budget holds for
  independent trades with normal tails; when trades come in streaks it does not hold
  (in our tests up to 48.5 % over 1,000 trades).
* Timing results compare your entries with random times in the same trading day. If your
  rules only allow entries at certain times, that comparison is too generous to chance.
* Statistics cannot tell whether the future will resemble the audited period.

## Reproduce
